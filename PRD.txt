1. PRODUCT SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Meet-N-Eat introduces people across the globe to one new person every
month. A member joins with one tap using LinkedIn (no separate password),
adds a few details, and is paired with someone of the same gender.

Every month starts with a check-in email: "Are you in this month?" Members
can stay in (or simply do nothing), take a break for the month, pause until
they're back, or opt out, with one tap from the email. Members who are in
can reveal their match on the site from the 8th (a plate flips over to show
who it is), and their match's details arrive by email later in the month.
The two members DM each other on LinkedIn and set up a relaxed 30-minute
call on whatever tool suits them both (Google Meet, Teams, Zoom). Grab a
coffee and a snack and get to know each other.

At most two emails a month: the check-in from the 3rd (everyone except
those who opted out) and the match details from the 10th (only members who
are in that month). Both are spread over the following days if the daily
sending limit is reached (14.4).


------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. GOALS AND NON-GOALS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Goals
- Make it easy and low-pressure to meet someone new outside your usual
  circles, anywhere in the world.
- Keep sign-up short: one tap with LinkedIn plus a few fields.
- Make the monthly rhythm predictable: check-in from the 3rd, reveal on
  the 8th, details from the 10th.
- Make pausing or leaving effortless and guilt-free.
- Work beautifully on phones first, then scale up to desktop.
- Never repeat a pair. Never show a match to anyone except the two members.

Non-goals (this release)
- In-app chat, calendar booking, or automatic scheduling.
- Public member directory or public reveal.
- Matching by location, industry, seniority or interests.
- Posting or sending LinkedIn connection requests on anyone's behalf.
- Translation. Members are asked to talk in English (see 10.4).

3. USERS AND CORE JOURNEYS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Visitor (not signed in)
Sees the landing page: hero image, "Your monthly match is behind the
plate.", a Register call to action (the plate), a lower-emphasis "Already
signed up? Log in", and three short "How it works" steps.

3.2 New member
1. Taps Register (the plate).
2. Taps "Continue with LinkedIn" and signs in on LinkedIn's own page with
   their usual LinkedIn email and password. Meet-N-Eat never sees the
   password.
3. Back on Meet-N-Eat, name, email and photo are already filled in from
   LinkedIn. They choose a gender, add role, company, LinkedIn profile URL
   and "Any fun, cool things about you?", confirm they're 18+, and tap
   Sign up.
4. Lands on Reveal Match, which says when their first match arrives.

3.3 Returning member
1. Taps "Log in", then "Continue with LinkedIn".
2. Lands on Reveal Match (the default tab).
3. From the 8th, taps the plate to reveal the match, opens their LinkedIn
   and sends a DM to set up a time.
4. Can look at Past Connects, leave feedback, change participation in
   Settings, edit their profile, refer a friend, or log out.

7. LINKEDIN SIGN-IN AND PROFILE DATA
------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 What LinkedIn gives us
- Sign In with LinkedIn using OpenID Connect, scopes: openid, profile,
  email.
- Returned: LinkedIn member ID, first and last name, verified email,
  photo. Not returned: job title, company, profile URL, age (see 23).

8. LOG IN AND ACCOUNT ACCESS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

- "Log in" → "Continue with LinkedIn". Members use their LinkedIn email
  and password on LinkedIn's page. There are no Meet-N-Eat passwords and
  no password-reset emails.
- Registered → Reveal Match. Not registered yet → the sign-up form.
- Sessions use secure, httpOnly cookies and last 30 days. Log out ends
  the session (it does not log them out of LinkedIn).
- Members who opted out can log in again and choose "Stay in" to rejoin.

14. MATCHING RULES AND THE MONTHLY CALENDAR
------------------------------------------------------------------------

14.1 Monthly calendar
Times are in each member's local time when we know it, otherwise UTC.

 When                      What happens
 ------------------------  -----------------------------------------------
 3rd → 9th, 09:00 local    Email 1, the check-in ("Are you in this
                           month?"), to everyone except members who opted
                           out. At most the daily limit per day (14.4);
                           most months everyone gets it on the 3rd.
 Until your pairing round  Members can stay in, take a break, pause or
                           opt out (from the email or Settings).
 7th, 10:00 UTC            Pairing round 1: everyone who is in and whose
                           check-in was sent before this moment. (10:00 UTC
                           on the 7th is when the 8th begins in the
                           earliest time zone.)
 8th, 00:00 local          Reveal opens on the site for round 1. Optional;
                           members can wait for the email.
 10th onwards, 09:00       Email 2, the match details (or the "no match"
 local                     note), only to members who are in this month.
                           Round 1 first, then round 2. At most the daily
                           limit per day; the rest roll over to the next
                           day, and so on.
 11th, 10:00 UTC           Pairing round 2, only if needed: members whose
                           check-in went out after round 1 (on the 7th–9th),
                           plus round-1 members left without a partner.
                           Their reveal opens straight away and their match
                           email follows in the queue.

Round 2 only happens when the check-in can't reach everyone before the 7th,
which means more than about 1,900 members on free Gmail.

14.2 Who is eligible in a given month
- Signed up with LinkedIn and the profile is complete.
- Status is Stay in (chosen or by default) for this month.
- Joined before round 1. Later sign-ups join next month.

14.3 Pairing rules
- Same gender option only.
- At most one match per member per month.
- Never pair the same two people again, ever.
- Find the largest possible set of new pairs in each gender group. Never
  fall back to a repeat pair.
- If someone has to sit out (odd number, or no new partner left), give
  priority next month to the people who sat out.
- Within those rules, pick pairs at random.
- The job is safe to re-run: it never creates a second match for the same
  member and month (database constraint plus a lock).
- Log per-group counts: eligible, matched, waiting.

14.4 Daily email limit and roll-over
- Gmail allows about 500 emails in any 24 hours. Scheduled emails use at
  most 480 a day (configurable), keeping a little headroom for retries.
- A job runs every hour. It picks members whose local time has just
  reached 09:00 on or after the send date, who haven't had this month's
  email yet, until the 24-hour total reaches 480. Anyone left over is sent
  the next day at 09:00 their time, and so on.
- Fairness: members who got the email later last month go first this
  month.
- Email 1 runs from the 3rd to the 9th: room for about 3,300 members.
  Email 2 starts on the 10th and keeps going day by day until everyone
  who is in has it (6 days to the 15th covers about 2,900 members).
- If Email 1 reaches someone on the 7th–9th (after round 1), it's still
  just the check-in. Their answer counts for this month and, if they're
  in, they're paired in round 2 and get their match later in the month.
- When membership gets close to these numbers, the admin view shows a
  warning; moving to Google Workspace (about 2,000 a day) or an email
  service is a settings change.

15. EMAILS (WITH DRAFT COPY)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

15.1 Rules
- Sent through Gmail SMTP using credentials kept in Vercel environment
  variables (see 23.E for limits).
- At most two emails per member per month:
    • Email 1, the check-in, from the 3rd: everyone except members who
      opted out (including members on a break or paused).
    • Email 2, the match details, from the 10th: only members who are in
      this month. Not sent to members on a break, paused or opted out.
      The "no match" note replaces Email 2 for members who are in but got
      no partner; it is not a third email.
- Both follow the daily limit and roll-over in 14.4.
- Every email has an HTML version and a plain-text version and a link to
  the site. Email 1 also carries Gmail's built-in one-click unsubscribe
  (counts as Opt out).
- Sending is idempotent: one email per member, month and type, even if a
  job is retried.
- Templates live in the code repository so they can be personalised
  automatically. A copy can also be saved as Gmail templates for
  reference, but Gmail's own templates can't be filled in automatically
  over SMTP.
- The copy below still needs a humanising pass (see Action items).

15.5 One-tap choices from the email
- Each button in Email 1 is a personal, signed link (it only works for
  that member and that month, and can only change their participation,
  nothing else).
- The link opens a small page: "Take a break this month?" with one
  "Confirm" button. One tap updates the database straight away and shows
  "Done! Enjoy your break ☕". No login needed.
- Why one confirm tap instead of zero: some work email systems
  automatically "click" every link in an email to scan it for viruses. If
  a link changed the database on its own, those scanners could opt people
  out by accident. The confirm tap prevents that.
- Gmail's own "Unsubscribe" button (shown next to the sender) is a true
  one-click opt-out; scanners can't trigger it.
- The member can always change the choice later in Settings.

16. DATA MODEL (NEON POSTGRES)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

members
  id (uuid, PK), linkedin_sub (unique; LinkedIn member ID),
  first_name, last_name, email (citext, unique; from LinkedIn),
  gender (enum: woman | man | non_binary | prefer_not_to_say),
  fun_facts (text), job_title, company, linkedin_url, photo_url,
  timezone (IANA name, default 'UTC'),
  age_confirmed_at, terms_accepted_at,
  status (enum: active | paused | left),
  referral_code (unique), referred_by (FK members),
  created_at, updated_at, deleted_at

member_months   -- one row per member per month
  member_id, period (date = first day of month),
  choice (enum: in | break), choice_source (default | email | site),
  chosen_at, round (1 | 2, nullable), checkin_sent_at
  primary key (member_id, period)

matches
  id (uuid, PK), period, round (1 | 2),
  member_low, member_high (FKs; low < high),
  created_at, revealed_at_low, revealed_at_high
  unique (member_low, member_high)          -- never repeat a pair
  unique (period, member) via helper table   -- one match per month

match_waitlist
  member_id, period (who sat out, for next-month priority)

email_log
  id, member_id, period, type (checkin | match | no_match),
  status (queued | sent | failed), attempts, sent_at, error
  unique (member_id, period, type)

feedback
  id, member_id, match_id (nullable), met (yes | not_yet | could_not),
  rating (1–5), comment, is_concern (bool), created_at

sessions
  managed by the auth/session library

17. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Decided: Vercel + Neon only. No Railway.
- Next.js (React) app on Vercel for the website and the API (route
  handlers).
- Neon Postgres with the Neon serverless driver. Connection string kept in
  Vercel environment variables.
- Auth: Sign In with LinkedIn (OpenID Connect) only, with httpOnly
  session cookies. No passwords stored.
- Email: Nodemailer over Gmail SMTP (App Password).
- Scheduled jobs: a free external scheduler (cron-job.org) calls one
  protected route every hour. That route sends whatever emails are due
  (14.4) and runs a pairing round when it's time (7th and, if needed,

  serverless time limits and picks up where it stopped. This works on

- Photos: copy the LinkedIn photo into Vercel Blob storage so it doesn't
  expire.
- Monitoring: Vercel logs plus a small admin view of job results.

23. FEASIBILITY NOTES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. Checking whether an email has a LinkedIn account. RESOLVED. LinkedIn
   has no way to look up whether an email belongs to an account, but we
   don't need one: members sign in with LinkedIn, so the email we get is
   LinkedIn's own verified email.

B. Fetching current job title and company. NOT AVAILABLE through the
   standard "Sign In with LinkedIn" (it gives name, email, photo and
   language only). Work history needs LinkedIn partner programmes, which
   require an application and approval. Decided: members type role and
   company at sign-up.

C. Profile photo from LinkedIn. FEASIBLE. Standard sign-in provides the
   photo URL. Copy it into our storage because LinkedIn photo links can
   expire.

D. LinkedIn profile link. NOT PROVIDED by standard sign-in. Members paste
   their linkedin.com/in/... URL; we check the format so the link on the
   match card always works.

E. Gmail SMTP. FEASIBLE with limits.
   - Needs 2-Step Verification on the Gmail account and an App Password
     (not the normal password).
   - Sending caps: about 500 recipients a day on a free Gmail account and
     about 2,000 a day on Google Workspace. Decided: spread sending over
     several days (14.4).
   - Bulk mail from a plain @gmail.com address is more likely to land in
     spam. A Google Workspace address on your own domain (for example
     hello@meet-n-eat.com) delivers better.
   - If you grow past these limits, switching to a transactional email
     service only means changing the sending settings.

F. One-tap choices from an email. FEASIBLE. Signed personal links update
   the database after one confirm tap, with no login (15.5).

G. Age check. LinkedIn does not share a member's age or birth date, and
   anyone can sign in with LinkedIn. Asking for a birth year doesn't prove
   anything either; real checks need ID documents. Decided: 18+, declared
   with a checkbox and backed by the Terms and the "Report a concern"
   option. 18+ is the safer choice because the product sets up private
   one-to-one calls between strangers; under-18s bring extra legal duties
   in many countries (for example parental consent rules for data in
   parts of the EU). This is not legal advice; check with a lawyer before
   launch if you can.
